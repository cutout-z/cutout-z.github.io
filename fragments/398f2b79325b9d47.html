Summary

At a 10% after-tax return, the cloud capital in service during each company's latest fiscal year earned 1.61 times what it needed. Capital in place today earns 0.97 times next year's requirement at today's revenue: revenue would need to rise 2%. Across the favourable and adverse allocation of costs the companies share with other businesses, the first figure runs from 1.34x to 2.12x and the second from 0.82x to 1.24x. The hurdle is an after-tax, unlevered return: the requirement is the pre-tax cash a year that delivers it once tax and the build lag are allowed for. This view treats operating leases as if they were capex, a memo of how the result would move; it is not the filed treatment.

How the three allocation scenarios are chosen

Companies that report capex, depreciation, rent and leases for the whole company, not for the cloud business, need a share of each assigned to the cloud business (the list below names them). One share per company moves every allocated figure together: capex, the depreciation add-back in the margin, operating rent, operating-lease additions, opening stock, leases not yet commenced and guided capex. The model evaluates each company's latest-year coverage at its low, middle and high share. Favourable uses the share with the highest coverage, adverse the lowest, central the middle one. Which end is favourable is computed, not assumed; a company with a single share is the same in all three. The high-share end allocates all of the company's capex to the cloud, so it overlaps the indirect line (capex for the owner's own use): do not add the two.

  • Google Cloud: favourable uses the low (14.6%) share, central mid (57.3%), adverse high (100.0%). allocation
  • Microsoft: favourable uses the low (41.5%) share, central mid (70.8%), adverse high (100.0%). allocation
  • Oracle: favourable uses the high (100.0%) share, central mid (75.2%), adverse low (50.5%). share of contribution only (filed cloud revenue share to whole company); capex, rent and leases are whole company

How the requirement is built

Each year's capex plus finance-lease additions is a vintage. It is split into IT equipment (life 4 years, build lag 0.5 years) and facilities (life 20 years, lag 1.5 years). Each part must earn the hurdle (10.0%, an after-tax unlevered return) over its life, after tax at 15% (0% for loss-makers) and the tax depreciation shield (5-year MACRS for servers, 100% bonus depreciation for vintages after 19 Jan 2025). The result is an equal annual charge per dollar, applied only while the asset is in service. Capital in place before the first year in scope is one opening block on its remaining life (assumption: half the bucket life).

Forecast to FY2030: what revenue would be needed

Revenue is not projected. For each future year, the revenue the cloud businesses would need for coverage of 1.00x at this hurdle, beside analyst consensus where it exists.

Assumption: capex is held flat after each company's guided year

Each company's capex is its own guidance for the guided year, then held at that level to the end of the forecast. That is an assumption, not a forecast: the one data provider found with analyst capex forecasts (Finnhub) charges for them, and no free source carries them, so none are used. If capex keeps rising after the guided year, the revenue needed rises with it.

  • Capex: company guidance for the guided year; after that held flat at the last guided level. The flat level is an assumption, marked as one wherever it is used. No analyst capex consensus is held in the inputs.
  • Revenue: held at the latest fiscal year, so "needed" is relative to today.
  • Committed rent: leases signed but not yet started, phased in evenly over the disclosed start window at total ÷ lease term (the timing is an assumption). The rent charged in a year is the run-rate at its midpoint. The total is scaled by the company's allocation share.
  • Years: year 1 is the fiscal year after each company's latest complete one.

How operating leases are treated

Operating leases are not capex here. Their cost is operating rent, deducted inside the margin, so the capital charge does not count them twice. Finance-lease additions are capex-equivalent and stay in capex. Everything below makes that visible: L1 rent as its own deduction, L2 operating-lease additions listed and excluded, L3 committed rent from leases not yet commenced as a separate band, L4 a memo of the result if operating leases were capex.

How to read this. In the memo, each operating-lease addition is annuitised over its lease term at the hurdle and the rent is taken out of the margin. The change in coverage is the hurdle-minus-lease-rate spread: the memo charges the hurdle (10.0%) on the leased assets where the lease contract charges only its own, lower, implied rate. Breakeven hurdle: 18.9% with rent deducted, 17.4% in the memo.

Lease term used for the memo: AWS 10.0 yr (assumption: no lease term disclosed); Google Cloud 13.5 yr (disclosed range for leases not yet commenced); Microsoft 10.5 yr (disclosed range for leases not yet commenced); Oracle 17.0 yr (disclosed range for leases not yet commenced); CoreWeave 11.5 yr (disclosed range for leases not yet commenced); Nebius 12.0 yr (disclosed maximum term (no range or average filed)); SpaceX AI 10.0 yr (assumption: no lease term disclosed).

Left out of the memo: SpaceX AI. Its rent is not disclosed, so there is no allocated rent to remove and no lease capex is added; its coverage is the same in both columns.

The memo is a what-if: rent and the capex treatment are never both applied, so no lease is counted twice.

System view (memo): what end customers pay for AI, beside capex

This memo is not driven by the controls above: it takes no hurdle, life or allocation scenario, and its figures are the same whichever are chosen.

The labs' own stated revenue from their end customers adds up to at least $73bn a year (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, xAI and X; on their latest statements and filings). Adding the AI run-rates stated by Microsoft AI business, Amazon AI business (stated on the AWS call) gives $135bn, but that can count the same dollars twice, because those run-rates may include what labs pay for compute and lab models resold through the clouds. On the other side, capex for the companies in the capex table below, for each company's latest guided year, is about $875bn. The two sides are not symmetric: that capex includes non-AI spending (no company discloses its AI share), and Alphabet, Meta, Oracle state no AI revenue to set against theirs. So a year of stated end-customer AI revenue is 8% to 15% of a year of capex.
