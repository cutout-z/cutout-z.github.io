Cockpit

Risk signals

A data centre earns its return from long leases to a few tenants. These signals follow the three things that can break that: the tenant cannot pay, the tenant does not renew, or more capacity is built than demand absorbs, or power costs more. Each row says what its latest reading shows, how far it moved and from what, and since when.

Market-level observations about participants in the Australian data-centre market, built from public data. Nothing here describes any lender's exposures or counterparties.

Terms used on this page
Hyperscalers
The global cloud operators that build and lease data-centre capacity at scale: Microsoft, AWS, Google Cloud, Oracle, Meta.
Frontier labs
The developers of the most capable AI models, now tenants in their own right: OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Mistral.
Neoclouds
Specialist providers renting dense GPU clusters: CoreWeave, Lambda, Crusoe, Nebius, Firmus.
Guarantor
A creditworthy party backstopping a weaker tenant's lease or debt.
Circular financing
A deal where the seller of compute or chips also funds the buyer, so the buyer's spending returns as the seller's revenue.
ARR
Annualised run-rate revenue: a lab's latest period of revenue scaled to a year, as it reports it. Not a forecast and not contracted.
Open-weights
A model whose parameters are published for anyone to run (Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen), as against a closed model served only by its developer.
NEM
The National Electricity Market: the grid of Queensland, NSW, Victoria, South Australia and Tasmania, run by AEMO.
