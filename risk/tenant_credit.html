Risk row

Tenant credit

Whether the tenant can pay the rent from its own cash flow and funding and, if it cannot, who stands behind it: the guarantors, and the circular deals that tie tenants to their funders. A DC owner/operator services its debt from lease income over terms of typically 15 years plus options, so the tenant's capacity to pay carries the structure. Tenants were historically hyperscalers and are increasingly frontier labs, neoclouds and labs with an NVIDIA guarantee. This row follows each tenant class, the financing loops that tie tenants to their funders and guarantors, and the leverage of the Australian operators themselves.

Nodes in this row

Each node has its own dossier: summary, evidence and the news it files.

Hyperscalers

Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Oracle and Meta: the anchor tenants against which long-dated DC leases have historically been written, and the baseline the rest of this row is read against. The node keeps that baseline in view so a change in it is noticed: capex taking a larger share of operating cash flow, free cash flow narrowing, or net cash moving towards net debt as the AI build is funded. A change in an anchor tenant's capacity to pay reaches every lease it holds, including those in Australia.

3 evidence pages 956 news items last activity 2026-10-09
Frontier labs

OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Mistral and the other model developers that sign multi-year compute and capacity commitments, directly or through a neocloud. A lab whose committed compute spend outruns its revenue is a weaker lessee, and a weaker counterparty to any guarantor behind it; funding rounds finance any gap, so their cadence and size are read alongside revenue. Open-weights progress cuts both ways: bearish for frontier-lab margins, which weakens the lab as a tenant (this node), and bullish for token volume and data-centre demand, which Renewal & obsolescence tracks.

2 evidence pages 1268 news items last activity 2026-10-05
Neoclouds & guarantors

CoreWeave, Lambda, Nebius and the other GPU clouds that lease DC capacity and rent compute on to labs and hyperscalers, and the guarantors, NVIDIA among them, who stand behind some of those leases. A neocloud pays from GPU rental income on hardware that ages faster than the lease runs, and from its own customers' payments. A guarantee moves the credit to the guarantor, whose fortunes depend on the same AI demand; the more leases share one guarantor, the more the market's credit concentrates in that name.

3 evidence pages 584 news items last activity 2026-10-02
Circular financing

Loops in which one company is funder, customer and guarantor at once: a chip vendor or cloud provider invests in a lab or neocloud, the money returns as spending on the investor's compute, and the investor or a related party backs the lease or the debt. The loop ties balance sheets together, so stress at one propagates through the lease to the others, and a guarantee from a party already exposed to the same tenant is worth less, because its capacity to pay falls when the tenant's does.

1 evidence page 318 news items last activity 2026-10-01
Australian operators

NEXTDC, Goodman, Macquarie Technology, AirTrunk, CDC, Equinix and the other owners and operators of Australian data centres, seen as a market: the companies that borrow against long-dated tenant leases. An operator's leverage, its funding cadence and the tenants its capacity is contracted to set how much lease income can fall before debt service is strained. The node follows them as market participants, through listed financials and announced raises, debt facilities and contracts.
