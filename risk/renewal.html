Renewal & obsolescence

The tenant reaches expiry or an option date and does not renew, because the facility no longer earns its return. A lease of 15 years plus options outlives several hardware generations and several turns in model pricing, so re-leasing, and the financing's tail with it, turns on two things: whether the tenant's AI spending pays back (inference economics, token prices and volume, hyperscaler capex against cloud revenue), and whether the shell can house the hardware of 2035–2045, measured against a stated tier-1 design envelope, never an actual facility.

Nodes in this row

Each node has its own dossier: summary, evidence and the news it files.

Model economics

What a unit of AI capability costs to buy and to serve: open versus closed models, price per million tokens at constant capability, and capability per dollar. AI workloads in Australian data centres are inference and cloud rather than training, so these inference-side economics bear on whether a tenant's workloads pay for the space it leases; the rate prices fall is the rate its revenue per token falls. Open-weights progress carries two signs: cheaper tokens get used, bullish for token volume and data-centre demand (this node), and bearish for frontier-lab margins, which Tenant credit tracks.

8 evidence pages 4 news items last activity 2026-08-14
Inference demand

How much AI work is actually being run: token volume, and consumer and enterprise adoption of AI services. AI workloads in Australian data centres are inference and cloud rather than training, so this is the demand a renewal depends on: a tenant renews capacity that paying workloads still fill. The token evidence here is OpenRouter traffic, which is developer and aggregator routing rather than enterprise cloud volume, so it reads as developer mindshare, not market share; frontier-lab revenue is the paid-adoption side.

2 evidence pages 0 news items last activity none
Hyperscaler AI ROI

Whether the hyperscalers' AI capital spending is paying back, read from what they disclose: whole-company capex against cloud-segment revenue and contracted backlog, and how capex guidance has moved from first issue to actual. A hyperscaler keeps leasing and renewing in Australia while its AI capex is paying back, so the gap between what it spends and what its cloud business brings in sits behind each option in a lease of 15 years plus options. Whether it can pay is Tenant credit; this node is whether it wants to stay.

3 evidence pages 49 news items last activity 2026-10-02
Hardware & facility

Whether the shell can house the hardware of 2035–2045. Accelerators turn over every year or two while a building is designed for decades, and successive generations have drawn more power per rack and moved cooling from air towards liquid; packaging, memory, interconnect and the supply chain behind them set the pace. An option in 2040 is exercised only if the shell can house the hardware of 2040, so the trajectory is measured against a stated tier-1 design envelope (AirTrunk/CDC class), never against an actual facility.

10 evidence pages 40 news items last activity 2026-10-02
Australian commitment

The anchor tenants' stated intent for Australia: hyperscaler investment announcements, site leads by evidence tier, and the megawatts in the project database linked to a hyperscaler. A tenant still committing capital and sites in Australia expects to need the capacity it leases there; a slowdown in announcements and leads would show before a slowdown in leasing, and so before a renewal is in doubt. Announcements are statements of intent, not contracted leases, and are read as such.
