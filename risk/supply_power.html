Supply, demand & power

Whether the Australian market absorbs the capacity being built, and what powering it costs. A facility leased for 15 years plus options renews into the market that exists at expiry. More capacity than demand absorbs slows lease-up of space built on spec and lets the tenant renew from strength; a change in the cost of power or the right to build, applied to existing assets, moves the tenant's return on the site. The nodes set the pipeline against demand and the grid, then cover power price and regulation.

Australian pipeline

Australian data-centre capacity from announced to operating, by region and status: risked MW, absorption, pre-commitment and workload mix. Capacity built against signed contracts carries renewal risk; capacity built on spec carries lease-up risk first. If absorbing what can actually be built needs demand growth faster than any published scenario, renewals are negotiated from weakness. Only pipeline with secured power can become competing supply. The workload mix shows how much of the market depends on AI inference rather than traditional and cloud demand.

3 evidence pages 405 news items last activity 2026-10-09
Demand & grid

Demand for data-centre power in Australia and the grid that serves it: AEMO's Electricity Statement of Opportunities, NEM demand by region, and the published data-centre consumption scenarios the pipeline is measured against. Demand that grows slower than the pipeline leaves a tenant more alternatives when its lease comes up for renewal; demand that outgrows it does the reverse. Grid headroom works on the supply side: where the grid has little spare capacity, less competing capacity can connect and run.

1 evidence page 64 news items last activity 2026-10-09
Power price

What powering a data centre in Australia costs: the delivered price by NEM region (wholesale energy plus network charges), how operators procure it (grid supply, power purchase agreements, on-site generation and storage), and how long new load waits to connect. Power is passed through to the tenant; a rising delivered price lowers the tenant's return on the site and is the lever a tenant pulls at renewal. The longer a new entrant waits to energise, the longer incumbents with connected power keep their pricing power.

1 evidence page 95 news items last activity 2026-10-02
Regulation & policy

The rules that set the cost of power and the right to build and own: AEMC rule changes, AER network determinations, AEMO connection procedures, state planning, foreign-investment review and government hosting certification. The question for each change is whether it reaches assets already built and leased. A grid-augmentation charge, behind-the-meter obligation, data-centre levy or planning restriction applied retrospectively to existing assets raises the cost of running a facility under a lease already signed, while the same instrument applied to new entrants only is a moat for incumbents with secured power.
