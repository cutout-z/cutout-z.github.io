Hyperscaler AI ROI

Whether the hyperscalers' AI capital spending is paying back, read from what they disclose: whole-company capex against cloud-segment revenue and contracted backlog, and how capex guidance has moved from first issue to actual. A hyperscaler keeps leasing and renewing in Australia while its AI capex is paying back, so the gap between what it spends and what its cloud business brings in sits behind each option in a lease of 15 years plus options. Whether it can pay is Tenant credit; this node is whether it wants to stay.

How it is measured
Formula
Capex ÷ cloud revenue = Σ capex (v_hyperscaler_capex) ÷ Σ cloud_revenue (ai_demand_disclosures.csv) over the four calendar quarters to the newest quarter that every hyperscaler with cloud revenue in the file has disclosed, %; the change is on the quarter before. Per ticker, at its newest disclosed quarter: capex ÷ cloud revenue; RPO or backlog ÷ capex over the four quarters to it; the disclosed AI run-rate and tokens processed (where given). A fiscal quarter counts in the calendar quarter in which it ends. No aggregate consumer AI spend is shown: it is not observable from disclosures. as_of = the newest capex period end (v_hyperscaler_capex.period) the value uses.
Inputs
data/reference/ai_demand_disclosures.csv: ticker, quarter, metric, value, unit, source_url, notes
data/db/ai_research.db: v_hyperscaler_capex
Refresh
Quarterly, after earnings; Hermes curates the disclosures · owner Hermes · stale after 120 days
Notes
Σ capex $477.0bn ÷ Σ cloud revenue $397.8bn across Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Oracle, 2025Q3 to 2026Q2, the newest quarter with capex and cloud revenue for all of them. 2026Q3 waits for Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet cloud revenue and capex (v_hyperscaler_capex); what is disclosed for it is in the table. Capex is as v_hyperscaler_capex records it, each company's cash purchases of property and equipment: assets taken on finance leases are not in it, and it is the whole company's, not its AI capex alone. Cloud revenue is the segment the file's notes name, so the ratio is a proxy for payback, not a return; Meta has no cloud revenue in the file and is in the table only. RPO / backlog is as each company reports it, so its scope differs: Microsoft's commercial RPO, Amazon's AWS backlog, Alphabet's Google Cloud backlog and Oracle's RPO. A few large contracts with frontier labs can make up much of a backlog; T1 (compute commitments, Frontier labs) carries them. Tokens, as the file's notes give them: Alphabet 2026Q2, “Gemini API: 22bn API tokens/minute (press release) = 31.7T/day; x30.44 days = ~964T/month”.
