Model economics

What a unit of AI capability costs to buy and to serve: open versus closed models, price per million tokens at constant capability, and capability per dollar. AI workloads in Australian data centres are inference and cloud rather than training, so these inference-side economics bear on whether a tenant's workloads pay for the space it leases; the rate prices fall is the rate its revenue per token falls. Open-weights progress carries two signs: cheaper tokens get used, bullish for token volume and data-centre demand (this node), and bearish for frontier-lab margins, which Tenant credit tracks.

How it is measured
Formula
Frontier = data_llm.pareto_front over blended price ((3 × input_price + output_price) ÷ 4, USD per 1M tokens) and GPQA, of the models released up to a date that carry a GPQA score and both prices; a model priced the same as a more capable one is not a member. Open share = members whose licence is neither proprietary nor unknown ÷ members of known licence (unknown left out and counted), at the snapshot's own date (_meta.updated, the as-of) and at each quarter end. The change is against the frontier of the models released by three months earlier. Cost gap = cheapest proprietary ÷ cheapest open-weights blended price among models at GPQA ≥ 75% (cheapest_frontier's threshold).
Inputs
data/reference/llm_leaderboard.json: _meta.updated, name, organization, license, release_date, input_price, output_price, gpqa_score
Refresh
ETL lane: ZeroEval leaderboard pull, every one to three weeks · owner ETL lane (ZeroEval) · stale after 45 days
Notes
105 of the snapshot's 400 models carry a GPQA score and both prices; on 02 Oct 2026, 13 sit on the frontier, 6 of them open-weights. Each quarter's frontier is rebuilt from the models released by its end at the snapshot's current prices, so a later price cut moves earlier quarters too; 6 models with an unknown licence are left out of the share and counted (0 of them on the frontier). At GPQA ≥ 75% the cheapest proprietary model, Seed 2.0 Mini at $0.175 per 1M tokens, costs 2.5× the cheapest open-weights one, GPT OSS 120B at $0.070.

How it is measured
Formula
P(t) = the cheapest blended price, (3 × input + output) ÷ 4 in $ per million tokens as data_llm.preprocess_ze blends it, among leaderboard models scoring at least 75% on GPQA and released by t (data_llm.cheapest_frontier's running minimum, each model at its snapshot price). Value = 100 × (P(as of) ÷ P(12 months earlier) − 1), the year-on-year change; quarter on quarter the same over 3 months. As of = the leaderboard's _meta.updated. The 2020–25 launch prices, blended the same way, carry no capability score: context on the chart only.
Inputs
data/reference/llm_leaderboard.json: _meta.updated, name, organization, release_date, gpqa_score, input_price, output_price
data/reference/token_prices_history.csv: date, model, provider, input_usd_per_mtok, output_usd_per_mtok · frozen, never aged
Refresh
ETL lane: ZeroEval leaderboard pull, every one to three weeks · owner ETL lane (ZeroEval) · stale after 45 days
Notes
Cheapest at ≥ 75% GPQA on 2 Oct 2026: $0.070/MTok (GPT OSS 120B, released 5 Aug 2025); a year earlier $0.070, three months earlier $0.070, so 0.0% year on year and 0.0% quarter on quarter. No new low in the last 3 months: the 3-month change is the year-ago price leaving the 12-month window, not a new price. Each model is at its price in this snapshot and dated by release, so the line is rebuilt at every pull; the 2020–25 launch prices carry no capability score and are context only.
